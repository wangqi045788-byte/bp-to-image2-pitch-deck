# Brand and visual system

Build the design language from the supplied company, not from a generic presentation theme.

## Evidence priority

1. supplied brand guide;
2. supplied logo and official assets;
3. official website or product pages;
4. supplied BP and company materials;
5. user-provided visual references;
6. restrained institutional fallback.

Record the evidence used for every brand choice. A reference visual may guide composition, density, typography roles, or material treatment; do not copy protected artwork or unrelated branding.

## Brand profile

Define:

- primary, secondary, accent, background, and text colors;
- typography roles and fallback families;
- grid, margin, corner, line, and spacing behavior;
- image treatment, icon semantics, chart language, and material cues;
- logo placement, clear space, and minimum size;
- approved and forbidden motifs;
- relationship between brand expression and institutional restraint.

## Hard visual rules

- Preserve the supplied logo exactly: no redrawing, recoloring, re-lettering, stretching, glow, bevel, or fabricated lockup.
- Preserve factual product images, interfaces, charts, and report excerpts exactly when they are used as evidence.
- Use one clear reading path and one dominant hierarchy.
- Default to no more than three major visual zones on an ordinary content page.
- Give one hero visual, evidence object, or conclusion clear dominance.
- Use one normal-width CJK sans-serif system per deck and a compatible Latin/numeric family. Limit type roles and weights.
- Copyfit before generation: shorten, restructure, or split content instead of shrinking to unreadable sizes.
- Use icons only when they encode meaning. Prefer diagrams, evidence, product imagery, or a strong spatial metaphor over decorative icon grids.

## Anti-generic gate

Reject the first direction if it could be reused unchanged for an unrelated company. In particular, avoid default blue-purple gradients, neon circuitry, glass cards, floating spheres, random 3D blocks, robot figures, generic city skylines, and dense card walls unless the source brand or user explicitly calls for them.

“Investment-bank quality” means accurate hierarchy, disciplined spacing, controlled contrast, credible evidence presentation, and selective visual ambition—not blandness and not decorative excess.
