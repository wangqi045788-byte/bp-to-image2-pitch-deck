# Narrative and slide selection

Choose the deck from the objective and evidence. Do not fill a fixed template.

## Selection sequence

1. Write the single sentence the audience should remember.
2. Group available evidence into two to five decision-relevant themes.
3. Identify the minimum sequence that makes the thesis credible.
4. Choose a slide archetype only when it advances the sequence and has enough evidence.
5. Move supporting detail to an appendix; delete decorative or repetitive pages.

## Common archetypes and triggers

| Archetype | Use when | Do not use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Every multi-slide deck | The deliverable is a single standalone analysis page |
| Executive thesis | Several themes must be understood together | It would merely repeat later slide titles |
| Problem / inflection | A documented pain point or market change drives urgency | Only generic industry claims are available |
| Product / solution | The mechanism and user value are supported | Features are aspirational or undefined |
| Architecture / workflow | A process, stack, or system relationship matters | A decorative flow adds no decision value |
| Use case | A concrete user, task, and outcome are evidenced | It requires invented customers or results |
| Market | Reliable scope, definition, and sizing exist | Only an unsourced TAM headline exists |
| Competition | Comparable dimensions and sources are defensible | It would imply unsupported superiority |
| Business model | Pricing, payer, or revenue logic is known | Monetization is speculative |
| Traction | Dated operating or commercial evidence exists | Metrics lack definitions or periods |
| Financials | Historical or forecast data and assumptions exist | Sparse inputs would force fabricated projections |
| Team | Relevant biographies are verified | Names or roles are incomplete or private |
| Roadmap | Milestones and status are clear | Targets may be mistaken for achievements |
| Investment highlights | Evidence supports a concise decision case | It becomes a generic list of adjectives |
| Risks / mitigants | The audience needs balanced decision support | Risks are boilerplate without company relevance |
| Appendix | Detail is useful but not essential to the main reading path | It hides a core caveat |

## Page-count behavior

- Respect an exact user count when feasible without padding or fabrication.
- For a range, choose the shortest complete narrative.
- For `auto`, let evidence density decide. A sparse input may produce three excellent pages; a well-supported transaction may justify fifteen.
- One page should have one decision message, not one source document section.

## Institutional writing

Use concise, evidence-led headlines. Prefer implications over topic labels. Keep bullet points parallel and specific. Avoid hype, vague superlatives, filler conclusions, and repetitive “AI-powered” language.

The deck should feel human-edited: controlled density, meaningful whitespace, varied but coherent page mechanics, and a deliberate change of pace between hero, evidence, diagram, and synthesis pages.
