# Presentation Research

Build the source record needed to decide what a presentation can responsibly say. Research questions may be narrow, and a user-provided primary source may be sufficient. Do not browse extensively when the brief asks only to reformat known content.

## Workflow

1. Extract the claims, dates, audience, geography, period and decisions that need evidence. Separate current facts from historical facts and the user's assertions.
2. Identify a primary-source target for each claim: issuer/regulator filings and official releases; government/statistical agencies for public statistics; original academic research or reputable review for academic claims; public procurement, standard or legal source for applicable formal requirements. Use reliable independent corroboration when interpretation or conflict warrants it.
3. Search exact names, definitions, date ranges and document titles. Open the source itself and record its title, publisher, publication/update date, URL or file path, relevant section/page/table, and access date. Search results alone are not evidence.
4. Extract only the material passage/value needed. Capture surrounding definition, population, time period, unit, limitations and whether values are actual, estimated, targeted or forecast.
5. Reconcile sources with different periods, scopes, currencies, definitions or revisions. Preserve each source claim and explain the chosen basis. Do not average incompatible estimates.
6. For derived figures, save the formula and input source IDs and reproduce the calculation. Label estimates and assumptions. Check any displayed number against its underlying record.
7. Deliver a compact, slide-ready evidence ledger with unresolved gaps and contrary evidence. Link each proposed factual slide claim to source IDs.

Read [source-method.md](references/source-method.md) for the ledger fields and source hierarchy. Pass the validated claim set to `ppt-analysis` to select methods and to `ppt-storyline` for slide mapping.

## Boundaries

- Do not upload or expose private source files to public web search, image services or unrelated external tools. Keep confidential material local and cite it internally by filename/page.
- Respect user-provided access restrictions and source rights. Attribute media and preserve brand/document context.
- Do not infer an absent fact from a polished deck, logo, image, proxy metric or search snippet.
- If a primary source cannot be found, report what was checked and downgrade the claim. Distinguish a source conflict from a fact error.
- Avoid collecting unrelated personal, contact or sensitive data about subjects.

This skill works alone. When `ppt-studio` is installed, keep source IDs intact into chart/table content, notes and the final delivery ledger.
