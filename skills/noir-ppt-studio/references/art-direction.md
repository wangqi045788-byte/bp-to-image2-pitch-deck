# Photographic editorial art direction

Apply this direction when the user chooses noir or this workflow. An explicit different template wins. Aim for the authority of a fashion editorial or art catalogue with the clarity of a business presentation.

## Visual grammar

**Photography leads.** Choose a purposeful crop: a person at one edge, craftsmanship close up, architecture with directional light, or a quiet product silhouette. Preserve skin, fabric and stone as real tonal information. Deep blacks must not erase the subject; avoid polished CGI masquerading as photography.

**Space is composed.** Place copy in naturally quiet image space or a deliberate paper/charcoal field. A 6–8% slide-width margin is a useful starting point, not a rigid rule. Let the subject determine image boundaries. Keep small text off faces and noisy fabric.

**Type has distinct jobs.** Pair a high-contrast display serif with restrained sans-serif body copy. Track only short labels. The supplied Bodoni Moda Editorial is optional; inspect its fine strokes in a real render. Choose a licensed, available body face and record substitutions. CJK needs an appropriate CJK font.

**Scale creates impact.** A cover can carry a huge short name; content pages need quieter headings. Do not apply cover scale everywhere. Separate large values and units when spacing crowds them. Never horizontally distort text to force a fit.

**Neutral tones carry the sequence.** Near-black, charcoal, paper white and neutral grey; photographic light supplies drama. Gold foil, smoke, silk waves, particles, chrome and glowing edges require a reason from the reference. They are not automatic luxury devices.

## Adaptable page patterns

1. Photographic cover: subject to one side, short serif name in quiet space, single subtitle, subordinate date/location.
2. Catalogue spread: one large photograph opposite a concise heading and two or three evidence statements, aligned labels and sources.
3. Metric page: one major value with readable unit/period, restrained chart or secondary image, one sentence of interpretation.
4. Analytical page: meaningful value chain, process, comparison or stakeholder diagram as the main visual. Photography is optional.
5. Quiet closing page: distinctive photograph, generous space and short closing line; reuse the cover only as an intentional echo.

These are options, not a fixed sequence. Avoid turning every interior into the same split layout. Full explanations and qualifications belong in notes when the canvas needs concise copy.

## Prompt scaffold

Tailor each prompt to the allocated geometry, for example:

> Photographic editorial image for a monochrome presentation: a patternmaker's hands precisely align dark wool and paper on an atelier table. Close framing, credible anatomy and tools, directional window light, tactile weave, restrained grain, neutral silver-grey highlights, deep readable shadows. Hands on the right; left third quiet and low contrast for a title. Landscape. No lettering, logos, decorative metal, surreal objects or glossy CGI. Conceptual craft illustration, not a documented company workshop.

Use an authentic source photo for a real company's workshop. For a whole-slide concept, supply exact copy and reference images; rebuild copy natively after acceptance.

## Review questions

- Is there one clear first impression followed by readable evidence?
- Does the picture support the subject, or only generic luxury?
- Are width, hairlines, spacing and baselines credible at full size?
- Is there excessive decoration, or insufficient visual substance?
- Is repeated imagery intentional and useful?
- Is an illustration being mistaken for documentary evidence?
- Does the exported PPT preserve the accepted composition and required text?

Repair the weakest specific element. A request for more impact does not imply permission to invent a new palette or pile up effects.
