# Analytical framework router

Choose a lens because it resolves the user's question with the available evidence. Several lenses can be combined if each adds a different decision. Do not run every framework for completeness.

| Question | Useful starting lens | Evidence to check | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What does this company do and how is it performing? | Business model, value drivers, customer/product segments, financial trend | Revenue/customer definitions, segment basis, operating/financial periods | Unsupported labels such as “leader” |
| What is happening in this market? | Defined market/value chain, demand drivers, supply, economics, regulation and competition | Geography, end-market definitions, original market-sizing method | Treating one TAM estimate as a verified total |
| Why does performance differ? | Driver tree, variance bridge, unit economics, cohort or process analysis | Definitions, comparable baseline, driver inputs, cohort dates | Claiming causal effects from correlation |
| Which strategy/option should be chosen? | Objectives and criteria, options, tradeoffs, scenario/sensitivity, implementation | Decision rights, constraints, evidence and uncertainty per option | Decorative SWOT with no effect on the choice |
| Is an investment/transaction attractive? | Thesis and countercase, cash flows/unit economics, valuation or returns, scenarios, catalysts and risks | As-of date, inputs, peers, calculation, basis and exit/discount assumptions | Presenting a sensitivity as an expected return |
| Where does an operation fail? | Value stream, bottleneck/root cause, capacity, quality, service and control | Process observation, demand/capacity, definitions, incidence/base rate | Unverified “root cause” from symptoms alone |
| Is product/customer behavior improving? | Journey/funnel, cohort/retention, experiment, segment comparison | Instrumentation, denominator, sample, treatment, duration and confounders | Equating association with an experiment |
| What does a study or literature establish? | Research question, conceptual model, methods, findings, limits and gaps | Primary paper, sample, design, citations and conflicting findings | Claiming consensus from one paper |
| What should a proposal/pitch secure? | Need, beneficiary, mechanism, proof, cost, alternatives, ask and milestones | Customer/user evidence, unit economics, delivery capability, terms | Converting aspiration into traction |
| How is a project or business period tracking? | Baseline versus actual, milestone/variance, risk, decisions and next steps | Approved plan, dated status, dependency, owner, change log | Treating planned or in-progress work as complete |
| What policy/programme is best? | Objectives, affected parties, status quo and options; impact, cost, feasibility and safeguards | Governing mandate, population, method, distributional effects, limits | Hiding losers, implementation costs or uncertainty |
| How should a topic be taught? | Learning objective, prerequisite, concept, worked example, practice and check | Syllabus and authoritative knowledge source | Filling slides with paragraphs that cannot teach |

## Familiar tools are conditional

- Porter Five Forces: industry structure and profit pressure; define the market and assess buyers/suppliers, substitutes, entrants and rivalry with evidence.
- PESTEL: external macro conditions that matter to a named company/decision and time horizon; omit immaterial factors.
- SWOT: concise synthesis only after strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats are independently supported; connect it to an option.
- Value chain: where work/cost/value occurs and how handoffs or differentiation change; define the organization's boundary.
- Market sizing: build from defined populations, units, adoption and prices; reconcile bottom-up and top-down where feasible.
- Root-cause tools: only when time-ordered or process evidence supports causal candidates; test competing causes.
- Valuation/comparables: use the appropriate date and normalized peers; bridge operating metrics, enterprise/equity value and share basis.

Do not force a matrix when narrative, a chart, comparison or decision tree is clearer. When evidence only supports a directional conclusion, use directional wording instead of a false precision score.
